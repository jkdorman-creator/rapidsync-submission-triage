Submission Triage Desk — shooting script

Two minutes thirty. Face at the top and the bottom, screen in the middle. Every word below is written to be spoken, not read.

Runtime 2:30 (cap is 3:00) Words ~370 Pace ~145 wpm Upload YouTube, public

Answering the format question first

Bookend your face. Don't run a corner cam.

Why not a persistent picture-in-picture: this UI is three dense columns with 12px labels, and YouTube will compress it. A corner cam covers roughly a tenth of the frame and pulls the eye away from the exact details judges need to read — the quoted lines from each document, the field notes, the reply. At three minutes there isn't enough runtime for on-camera presence to pay for that cost.

Why show your face at all: your unfair advantage in this contest is that you actually do this job. Three of the four criteria are about the work, but Potential Impact is scored on whether the case is credible — and a real underwriter saying "this is my week" is credibility a screencast cannot buy. That lands in the first fifteen seconds. It doesn't need to persist.

So: full-frame face for the open, hard cut to full-screen demo, full-frame face for the close. Two cuts, no overlay.

The script

0:00
Face, full frame

Sitting, plain background. No slide, no title card — start talking immediately.

I'm Justin, and I place workers' comp insurance. A submission is an email with two PDFs attached. Somebody reads it, pulls fifteen numbers out of it, and decides what happens. Forty times a week. Agents say the retyping is the worst part of the job.

Doing the work of: Potential Impact. Establishes you as the practitioner before anything technical.

0:18
Screen — top of the page

Cut to the desk, already loaded, nothing playing. Cursor still. Let the header and the "What your agent cannot do" panel be visible.

This is our submission desk. It hands eight tools to the AI agent in my browser through WebMCP. It has no password of its own — I signed in, it works inside my session. It can't route anything, and it can't send anything.

Doing the work of: WebMCP Leverage, and the credential argument. Say "no password of its own" clearly; it's the line judges remember.

0:37
Click "One with a problem"

Click, then get the cursor out of the way. Let it run.

Watch. It opens the email, and then it does the thing a chat window can't — it asks my page to open the PDFs. Those files are here, not in the model.

0:49
Record fills; risk summary lands

Stay wide enough to catch the record and the risk strip together. Don't scroll while it's writing.

The record fills in, and every value is tagged with which document it came from. Two point seven million in payroll, fifty-five employees, mod one point one eight.

Doing the work of: Execution. This is the most visually satisfying twelve seconds in the video — don't rush it.

1:01
The contradiction card

Scroll so the contradiction card fills the right third. Pause a full second before speaking.

Then it stops. The application says no prior losses. The loss run shows four claims and ninety-two thousand dollars, including an amputation. Both documents are in the file, and it can't tell you which one is right.

1:16
Hover the two quoted lines, then the buttons

Move the cursor slowly across the two quotes as you name them. Then click Use the loss run.

So it doesn't guess. It puts the actual line from each document on screen, and two buttons. That call is mine. One click, and both answers stay on the file — so the disagreement never quietly disappears.

Doing the work of: Creativity & Ambition. This is the moment the entry is really about.

1:31
Cursor rests on the blank FEIN field

Point at the amber dashed FEIN box. It should still be empty — that's the point.

The FEIN is a different kind of problem. It's blank in both documents, so I don't have it either. It doesn't ask me for it. It puts it on the list for the producer.

Doing the work of: the detail that says a practitioner built this. Most demos would have had you type it.

1:43
Approve routing → the drafted reply

Click Approve and route. Let the reply draft itself and scroll into view. Don't send it on camera.

It proposes an indication, with its reasoning. I approve it. Then it writes the email back to the producer — the indication, and a numbered list of exactly what we need to turn it into a quote. It can't send that either.

1:57
Fast: click "A clean one", then "One we cannot write"

Speed these up in the edit — 2× is fine. Land on the green Quote Now chip, then the red Likely Decline chip.

The rules underneath are real, not decoration. A clean one goes straight to Quote Now. And this roofer — where the producer is pushing hard for a Friday answer — is out in twenty seconds, because the class code is prohibited. The deadline doesn't change the answer.

Doing the work of: proving the four lanes discriminate. Without this, a judge assumes one hard-coded path.

2:13
Face, full frame

Cut back. Slower delivery than the open. Stop talking when you're done — no sign-off, no "thanks for watching".

None of this works without WebMCP. The agent can't open a PDF sitting on my page. It doesn't know my appetite rules. And every other way to automate this job needs my carrier password — this one never had one. It gathers. I decide.

Doing the work of: the explicit "how you used WebMCP" the rules require, in the last thing they hear.

Before you record

  • Record at 1920×1080 or larger. Set browser zoom to 110–125% so the 12px labels survive YouTube's compression.
  • Use a clean browser profile — no bookmarks bar, no extensions, no other tabs, no profile avatar.
  • Hit Reset between takes so the transcript and record start clean.
  • Move the cursor deliberately and slowly. Wandering reads as nervous; parking it reads as confident.
  • Record audio separately if you can, and lay it over the screen capture. Trying to narrate live while clicking is where most demo videos fall apart.
  • Do the two face segments in one sitting, same shirt, same light, so the cut back at 2:13 doesn't jar.

Rules that can disqualify you — check before uploading

No third-party trademarks and no copyrighted music. Your demo is clean — every carrier, agency and insured is invented — but nothing else can be on screen. No real carrier portals, no logos in a browser tab, no background music unless it's licensed to you. Silence under the narration is fine and safer.

Under three minutes, on YouTube, publicly visible, in English, with audio that covers what you built and how you used WebMCP. The script above is 2:30, which leaves room to breathe.
